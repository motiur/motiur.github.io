Machine Learning for Agriculture and Climate

Machine learning for food and climate, with Bangladesh as the running example: plant disease detection, remote sensing and crop mapping, yield prediction, weather forecasting and ML weather models, floods, downscaling, extremes and decisions, farm sensors, and validation that respects space and time.

  • Remote sensing
  • Crop mapping
  • Weather forecasting
  • Floods
  • Extremes
  • Spatial validation

Sessions

  1. 01

    Machine learning for agriculture and climate

    Map agriculture and climate problems to machine learning tasks and data, and measure how random cross-validation misleads on a district-by-year yield panel compared with leave-one-year-out and leave-region-out validation.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  2. 02

    Plant disease detection from leaf images

    Train a small CNN to classify leaf diseases, show that it learns shortcuts from lab photos, and make it more robust in the field and willing to abstain when unsure.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 2 NOT RUN cells
  3. 03

    Seeing crops from space

    Compute vegetation and water indices, mask clouds, build composites, and smooth a cloudy NDVI time series to estimate crop green-up dates.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  4. 04

    Crop type mapping

    Classify crop types from satellite time series with a random forest and a 1-D CNN, and validate the map with random, field-level and spatial block cross-validation.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  5. 05

    Predicting crop yield

    Build linear and gradient-boosting yield models from weather and satellite features, validate them on unseen years and an unusually hot year, and give prediction intervals with quantile regression.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  6. 06

    Weather data, baselines and forecast skill

    Build persistence and climatology baselines, score temperature forecasts with RMSE, skill scores and anomaly correlation, and evaluate probabilistic forecasts with CRPS, the Brier score and a reliability diagram.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  7. 07

    Machine learning weather prediction

    Simulate a chaotic toy atmosphere (Lorenz-96), train a small periodic CNN to step it forward 6 hours, roll it out to 25 days against persistence, climatology and a perfect model, and see how MSE training blurs long forecasts and how an ensemble measures its own uncertainty.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 2 NOT RUN cells
  8. 08

    Floods: mapping and forecasting

    Map flood water in synthetic radar images with Otsu's threshold and a small U-Net, score the maps with pixel accuracy and IoU, test them under a calibration shift, and compare an LSTM with a linear-reservoir model for river flow using the Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 2 NOT RUN cells
  9. 09

    Downscaling and bias correction

    Bias-correct synthetic climate-model temperature and rainfall with the delta method, quantile mapping and quantile delta mapping, check whether each keeps the model's future change, then downscale coarse rain fields with bicubic interpolation and a small CNN and compare them on RMSE and on the 99th percentile.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  10. 10

    Extremes, uncertainty and decisions

    Fit a generalized extreme value distribution to synthetic annual maximum rainfall, compute 10-, 50- and 100-year return levels with bootstrap intervals, and decide when to act on probabilistic forecasts with the cost–loss model, a reliability diagram and the economic value of a forecast.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  11. 11

    Sensors, soil and water on the farm

    Simulate a soil-moisture sensor with noise, spikes, gaps and drift, clean it with a moving average and a Kalman filter, detect spikes and drift, and compare irrigation decisions made from raw and cleaned readings by water used and days of crop stress.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  12. 12

    Responsible machine learning for farmers and the climate, and the capstone

    Estimate the energy and CO2 of training and running models from stated assumptions, turn a rain probability into Bangla advice with honest uncertainty wording, compare the value of personal and one-size advice for farmers with different costs and losses, and write a model card.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell

Before you start

One notebook per session. Read them with their saved outputs on the course website or on GitHub, or open them in Google Colab or Kaggle Notebooks and run them from the top.

  • Every cell without a NOT RUN label was run once, top to bottom, on a CPU in November 2026 (Python 3.13, NumPy 2.5, SciPy 1.18, pandas 3.0, scikit-learn 1.9 and matplotlib 3.11, plus PyTorch 2.15 in sessions 2, 4 and 7–9), and the outputs are saved in the notebooks. A second, independent run reproduced every printed number; only timings differ.
  • All lab data are synthetic, generated in the notebooks to behave like the real thing: multispectral image time series with clouds, crop-phenology curves, district yield panels, weather series, a Lorenz-96 "atmosphere", radar images with speckle, climate-model output and farm sensor streams. Each notebook says which real dataset its data stand in for, and a NOT RUN cell shows how to load that dataset (ERA5 from the Copernicus Climate Data Store, Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 from public STAC catalogues, PlantVillage, Sen1Floods11, WeatherBench 2, CMIP6). Synthetic data make the numbers illustrations, not findings about Bangladesh.
  • Cells marked TODO are exercises for students. NOT RUN cells need a download, an account or a library that is not installed here (the table below says what each needs). They were written from the libraries' documented interfaces and have not been executed, so run each one before recording.
  • Nothing extra needs installing for the runnable cells on Google Colab or Kaggle.
  • Results that went against expectation are stated in each notebook's "What to notice" cell and in the course document. Examples: a leaf mask did worse than randomizing the background (session 2); smoothing did not improve green-up dates (session 3); even the linear model's 90% prediction intervals covered only 88% of the yields in held-out years (session 5); the rain-probability forecast had little skill (session 6); a model trained to forecast five days directly was worse than rolling out a one-step model until day 10 (session 7); a fixed radar threshold beat Otsu's method, and the CNN fell below Otsu on darker scenes (session 8); quantile delta mapping kept the change in heavy rain but not in the mean (session 9); deciding from a Kalman filter without drift correction caused the most crop-stress days (session 11).
