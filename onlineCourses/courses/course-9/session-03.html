1. The 2026 work behind this lab¶

Chen et al., "Exploration vs Exploitation: Rethinking RLVR through Clipping, Entropy, and Spurious Reward" (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2512.16912) asks why two opposite-looking tricks both seemed to help reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR): rewards unrelated to correctness ("spurious" rewards), and pushing the policy's entropy down. The authors report that the clipping in PPO-style objectives, under spurious rewards, lowers policy entropy, and that lowering entropy alone does not reliably improve performance. Two 2025 papers set up the debate: Yue et al. (arXiv 2504.13837) found that RLVR models beat their base models at pass@1 but lose at pass@k for large k, and Shao et al. (arXiv 2506.10947) found that random rewards could raise maths scores for some models. Two 2026 preprints try to keep exploration alive: DSDR (Wan et al., arXiv 2602.19895) and ProGRPO (Li et al., arXiv 2602.05281).

This lab is a toy illustration, not a reproduction. The "model" is a table of preferences over six solution strategies for four kinds of problem, not a language model, and one action stands for a whole solution. It can show how group-normalized advantages, clipping and entropy interact; it cannot show what happens inside a real model's chain of thought.

2. A toy reasoning task with verifiable answers¶

There are 4 types of problem and 150 problems of each type. A solver can try one of 6 strategies. For each type, one strategy solves 70% of its problems, two others solve 35% and 25%, and the rest solve 15%; which problems a strategy solves is random. The reward is verifiable: 1 if the chosen strategy solves the problem, else 0.

The policy is a 4 × 6 table of logits: for each problem type, a softmax over strategies. We start from two "base models": one whose favourite strategy is the best one for each type (a good prior) and one whose favourite is a weak strategy (a bad prior). For a problem solved with probability $p$ per attempt, pass@k $= 1-(1-p)^k$ is the chance that at least one of $k$ attempts succeeds.

N_TYPES, N_STRAT, N_PER = 4, 6, 150
gen = np.random.default_rng(1)
types = np.repeat(np.arange(N_TYPES), N_PER)
rate = np.full((N_TYPES, N_STRAT), 0.15)
for t in range(N_TYPES):
    rate[t, t], rate[t, (t + 1) % N_STRAT], rate[t, (t + 2) % N_STRAT] = 0.70, 0.35, 0.25
solves = torch.tensor(gen.random((len(types), N_STRAT)) < rate[types], dtype=torch.float32)   # problem x strategy
types_t = torch.tensor(types)

def base_policy(prior):
    g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(0)
    z = torch.randn(N_TYPES, N_STRAT, generator=g) * 0.3
    for t in range(N_TYPES):
        z[t, t if prior == "good" else (t + 3) % N_STRAT] += 2.0      # the base model's favourite strategy
    return z

def metrics(z, favourite=None):
    with torch.no_grad():
        P = torch.softmax(z, -1)
        p = (P[types_t] * solves).sum(-1)                              # per-problem success probability
        out = {"entropy": -(P * P.log()).sum(-1).mean().item(), "pass@1": p.mean().item(),
               "pass@8": (1 - (1 - p) ** 8).mean().item(), "pass@64": (1 - (1 - p) ** 64).mean().item()}
        if favourite is not None:
            out["p(favourite)"] = P[torch.arange(N_TYPES), favourite].mean().item()
        return out

print(f"problems that at least one strategy solves (the ceiling for pass@k): {(solves.max(1).values > 0).float().mean():.3f}")
for prior in ["good", "bad"]:
    m = metrics(base_policy(prior))
    print(f"base model with a {prior} prior: " + ", ".join(f"{k} {v:.3f}" for k, v in m.items()))

3. GRPO with PPO clipping, and the collapse of entropy¶

Each step draws 64 problems and samples a group of $G = 8$ strategies for each. The advantage of sample $i$ is its reward minus the group mean, divided by the group standard deviation (GRPO; Course 3, session 7). The update maximises the clipped objective $\min(r_i A_i,\ \mathrm{clip}(r_i, 1-\epsilon_{low}, 1+\epsilon_{high}) A_i)$, where $r_i$ is the ratio of new to old probability, for 8 gradient steps on the same samples. We track the metrics for 150 steps.

def train(z0, reward="true", eps_low=0.2, eps_high=0.2, clip=True, ent_coef=0.0, kl_coef=0.0,
          steps=150, G=8, M=64, epochs=8, lr=3.0, seed=0):
    g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(seed)
    z = z0.clone().requires_grad_(True)
    opt = torch.optim.SGD([z], lr=lr)
    ref = torch.log_softmax(z0, -1)
    favourite = z0.argmax(-1)
    hist, clip_frac, clip_fav = [metrics(z, favourite)], [], []          # clip_fav: share of samples at the lower bound
    for step in range(steps):
        idx = torch.randint(len(types), (M,), generator=g)
        with torch.no_grad():
            logp_old_all = torch.log_softmax(z, -1)[types_t[idx]]
            a = torch.multinomial(logp_old_all.exp(), G, replacement=True, generator=g)       # M x G sampled strategies
            if reward == "true":
                r = solves[idx].gather(1, a)                                                     # verifiable reward
            else:
                r = (torch.rand(M, G, generator=g) < 0.5).float()                                # spurious: a coin flip
            adv = (r - r.mean(1, keepdim=True)) / (r.std(1, keepdim=True) + 1e-6)
            logp_old = logp_old_all.gather(1, a)
        for e in range(epochs):
            logp_all = torch.log_softmax(z, -1)
            ratio = torch.exp(logp_all[types_t[idx]].gather(1, a) - logp_old)
            if clip:
                obj = torch.min(ratio * adv, torch.clamp(ratio, 1 - eps_low, 1 + eps_high) * adv)
            else:
                obj = ratio * adv
            loss = -obj.mean()
            if ent_coef:
                loss = loss + ent_coef * (logp_all.exp() * logp_all).sum(-1).mean()            # minus entropy
            if kl_coef:
                loss = loss + kl_coef * (logp_all.exp() * (logp_all - ref)).sum(-1).mean()     # KL to the base model
            if e == epochs - 1:
                with torch.no_grad():
                    active = max((adv != 0).float().sum().item(), 1)
                    clip_frac.append(((ratio > 1 + eps_high) & (adv > 0)).float().sum().item() / active)   # hit the upper bound
                    clip_fav.append(((ratio < 1 - eps_low) & (adv < 0)).float().sum().item() / active)     # hit the lower bound
            opt.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); opt.step()
        hist.append(metrics(z, favourite))
    return hist, float(np.mean(clip_frac)), float(np.mean(clip_fav)), z.detach()

def summary(hists, key):
    vals = np.array([h[-1][key] for h in hists])
    return vals.mean(), vals.std(ddof=1) / np.sqrt(len(vals)) if len(vals) > 1 else 0.0

z_good = base_policy("good")
runs_grpo = [train(z_good, seed=s) for s in range(3)]
hists = [r[0] for r in runs_grpo]
base_m = metrics(z_good)
for key in ["entropy", "pass@1", "pass@8", "pass@64"]:
    print(f"{key:8s} base {base_m[key]:.3f} -> after GRPO {summary(hists, key)[0]:.3f} (mean of 3 seeds)")

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.2))
for h in hists:
    axes[0].plot([m["entropy"] for m in h], c="C0", alpha=0.7)
    axes[1].plot([m["pass@1"] for m in h], c="C1", alpha=0.7)
    axes[1].plot([m["pass@64"] for m in h], c="C2", alpha=0.7)
axes[0].set_title("policy entropy collapses"); axes[0].set_xlabel("training step"); axes[0].set_ylabel("entropy (nats)")
axes[1].set_title("pass@1 (orange) rises, pass@64 (green) falls"); axes[1].set_xlabel("training step")
axes[1].set_ylabel("rate")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

4. Spurious rewards: what does clipping do when the reward is a coin flip?¶

Now the reward ignores correctness: each sample gets 1 with probability 0.5. The advantages are pure noise with mean zero, so any systematic change in the policy comes from the update rule itself. We compare clipping on and off, for both base models, over 8 seeds each, and report the mean ± standard error. p(favourite) is the probability the policy gives to the base model's favourite strategy.

spurious = {}
print("base prior | clipping | entropy        | pass@1         | pass@64        | p(favourite)")
for prior in ["good", "bad"]:
    z0 = base_policy(prior)
    m0 = metrics(z0, z0.argmax(-1))
    print(f"{prior:10s} | start    | {m0['entropy']:.3f}          | {m0['pass@1']:.3f}          | {m0['pass@64']:.3f}          | {m0['p(favourite)']:.3f}")
    for clip in [True, False]:
        hs = [train(z0, reward="random", clip=clip, seed=s)[0] for s in range(8)]
        spurious[(prior, clip)] = hs
        cells_txt = " | ".join(f"{summary(hs, k)[0]:.3f} ± {summary(hs, k)[1]:.3f}" for k in ["entropy", "pass@1", "pass@64", "p(favourite)"])
        print(f"{prior:10s} | {'on ' if clip else 'off'}      | {cells_txt}")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 3.2))
for (prior, clip), hs in spurious.items():
    ent = np.mean([[m["entropy"] for m in h] for h in hs], axis=0)
    ax.plot(ent, ls="-" if clip else "--", c="C0" if prior == "good" else "C3",
            label=f"{prior} prior, clipping {'on' if clip else 'off'}")
ax.set_xlabel("training step"); ax.set_ylabel("entropy (nats), mean of 8 seeds")
ax.set_title("Random rewards: entropy still falls")
ax.legend(fontsize=8)
plt.show()

5. Keeping exploration alive¶

Three simple ways to slow the collapse in section 3, all with the true reward and the good prior:

  • clip-higher: a larger upper clip bound ($\epsilon_{high} = 0.28$, $\epsilon_{low} = 0.2$), so rare strategies with positive advantage can grow faster (the idea behind DAPO's Clip-Higher, Yu et al. 2025);
  • entropy bonus: add $0.3 \times$ entropy to the objective;
  • KL anchor: subtract $0.5 \times \mathrm{KL}(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{base})$, which keeps the policy near the base model.

Before trusting any conclusion about clipping, check whether the clip is active. The last two columns give the share of samples (with non-zero advantage, averaged over training) whose ratio was beyond the upper bound with a positive advantage, or beyond the lower bound with a negative advantage, on the last inner step. Clip-higher can only matter through the first of these.

variants = {"GRPO (clip 0.2)": {}, "clip-higher (0.2 / 0.28)": dict(eps_high=0.28),
            "entropy bonus 0.3": dict(ent_coef=0.3), "KL anchor 0.5": dict(kl_coef=0.5)}
final_runs = {}
print("variant                   | entropy at step 20 | entropy | pass@1 | pass@8 | pass@64 | hit upper bound | hit lower bound")
for name, kw in variants.items():
    runs = runs_grpo if name.startswith("GRPO") else [train(z_good, seed=s, **kw) for s in range(3)]
    final_runs[name] = runs
    hs = [r[0] for r in runs]
    vals = " | ".join(f"{summary(hs, k)[0]:.3f}" for k in ["entropy", "pass@1", "pass@8", "pass@64"])
    ent20 = np.mean([r[0][20]["entropy"] for r in runs])
    print(f"{name:25s} | {ent20:.3f}              | {vals} | {np.mean([r[1] for r in runs]):.4f}          | {np.mean([r[2] for r in runs]):.4f}")

ks = np.arange(1, 65)
def pass_curve(z):
    p = (torch.softmax(z, -1)[types_t] * solves).sum(-1).numpy()
    return np.array([np.mean(1 - (1 - p) ** k) for k in ks])

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 3.2))
ax.plot(ks, pass_curve(z_good), "k--", label="base model")
for name in ["GRPO (clip 0.2)", "entropy bonus 0.3", "KL anchor 0.5"]:

# NOT RUN: needs a GPU and `pip install trl transformers datasets` (downloads a small open model and GSM8K)
import re
from datasets import load_dataset
from trl import GRPOConfig, GRPOTrainer

data = load_dataset("openai/gsm8k", "main", split="train[:2000]")
data = data.map(lambda ex: {"prompt": ex["question"] + "\nEnd with the final answer after ####.",
                            "answer": ex["answer"].split("####")[-1].strip()})

def correct(completions, answer, **kwargs):                 # verifiable reward: 1 if the final number matches
    out = []
    for c, a in zip(completions, answer):
        m = re.findall(r"####\s*(-?[\d,\.]+)", c)
        out.append(1.0 if m and m[-1].replace(",", "") == a.replace(",", "") else 0.0)
    return out

args = GRPOConfig(output_dir="grpo-toy", num_generations=8, max_completion_length=256, learning_rate=1e-6,
                  per_device_train_batch_size=8, logging_steps=10,
                  epsilon=0.2, epsilon_high=0.28)            # epsilon_high is clip-higher; check that your TRL version has it
trainer = GRPOTrainer(model="Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct", reward_funcs=correct, args=args, train_dataset=data)

What to notice¶

  • With the true reward, GRPO raised pass@1 from 0.465 to 0.731 but lowered pass@64 from 0.914 to 0.735, while entropy fell from 1.471 to 0.009 nats: each problem type collapsed onto its single best strategy. This is a toy version of what Yue et al. report for real models (better at k = 1, worse at large k). Here the collapse is even the best choice for pass@1, because one strategy is best on average for each type.
  • With random rewards, entropy still fell in all four settings, and it fell further with clipping: 0.854 against 1.032 for the good prior and 0.805 against 1.020 for the bad prior, differences of about two combined standard errors, so suggestive rather than certain with 8 seeds. With clipping the policy also stayed closer to its base favourite (p(favourite) 0.441 against 0.234, and 0.555 against 0.295). This matches the direction Chen et al. report.
  • Lower entropy did not mean better results. With clipping, pass@1 stayed at 0.448 (it started at 0.465) and 0.208 (from 0.205), and pass@64 fell in every random-reward run. Without clipping the policy drifted away from its favourite, so pass@1 fell for the good prior (0.335) and rose a little for the bad prior (0.255). The toy does not reproduce Shao et al.'s report that random rewards can raise accuracy.
  • Clip-higher made no measurable difference: entropy at step 20 was 0.081 against 0.082, and the final numbers were identical, even though it cut the samples stopped at the upper bound from 1.14% to 0.11%. In this toy the collapse comes from real differences in reward, not from the clip. Check that a mechanism is active before you credit or blame it.
  • The entropy bonus (pass@1 0.623, pass@64 0.901) and the KL anchor (pass@1 0.663, pass@64 0.884) kept most of the base model's coverage but gave up part of the pass@1 gain. In this toy, exploration and pass@1 trade against each other. The 2026 methods (DSDR, ProGRPO) aim to keep both; this toy cannot tell whether they succeed.
