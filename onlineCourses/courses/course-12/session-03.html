1. By hand first: reading speed and a tiny alignment¶

  • Reading speed. A caption block reads "Second, they must appear at the right time," and stays on screen for 2.0 seconds. Count its characters, spaces included. How many characters per second (cps) must the viewer read? If your limit is 17 cps, how long must the block stay up?
  • Dynamic time warping (DTW). Align the sequences x = 1 3 4 3 and y = 1 1 3 4 4 3 with cost $|x_i - y_j|$. DTW fills a table $D(i, j) = |x_i - y_j| + \min(D(i-1, j),\ D(i, j-1),\ D(i-1, j-1))$ and the best total cost is $D(4, 6)$. What is it, and which frames of y does each frame of x match?

def dtw(x, y, dist=lambda a, b: np.abs(a - b), band=None):
    # returns the total cost, the path [(i, j), ...] and the number of table cells computed
    n, m = len(x), len(y)
    D = np.full((n + 1, m + 1), np.inf); D[0, 0] = 0.0
    cells = 0
    for i in range(1, n + 1):
        centre = (i - 1) * (m - 1) / max(n - 1, 1)                     # the diagonal of a non-square table
        lo, hi = (1, m) if band is None else (max(1, int(np.ceil(centre - band)) + 1), min(m, int(centre + band) + 1))
        for j in range(lo, hi + 1):
            D[i, j] = dist(x[i - 1], y[j - 1]) + min(D[i - 1, j], D[i, j - 1], D[i - 1, j - 1])
            cells += 1
    i, j, path = n, m, [(n - 1, m - 1)]
    while (i, j) != (1, 1):                                            # walk back along the cheapest predecessors
        steps = [(D[i - 1, j - 1], i - 1, j - 1), (D[i - 1, j], i - 1, j), (D[i, j - 1], i, j - 1)]
        _, i, j = min(steps)
        path.append((i - 1, j - 1))
    return D[n, m], path[::-1], cells

cost, path, _ = dtw(np.array([1, 3, 4, 3.0]), np.array([1, 1, 3, 4, 4, 3.0]))
print(f"DTW cost {cost:.0f}; path (x frame, y frame): {path}")

2. DTW on two "recordings" spoken at different speeds¶

Dynamic time warping (Sakoe & Chiba 1978) aligns two sequences that follow the same course at different speeds, for example two recordings of the same sentence. Here the reference is a synthetic 2-D feature track (think of two formant frequencies) 120 frames long. The second "speaker" says the same thing in 160 frames, slowly in the middle, with noise. Because we made the warp ourselves, we know the true match for every frame and can measure the error. A Sakoe–Chiba band only fills cells near the diagonal: faster, and it forbids extreme warps.

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
N, M = 120, 160
tr = np.linspace(0, 1, N)
ref = np.stack([np.sin(2 * np.pi * 2 * tr) + 0.6 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 5 * tr),
                np.cos(2 * np.pi * 3 * tr) * np.exp(-2 * tr)], axis=1)          # (120, 2) feature track
u = np.linspace(0, 1, M)
warp = u + 0.12 * np.sin(np.pi * u) * np.sin(2 * np.pi * u)                    # monotone time warp: query time -> reference time
true_i = warp * (N - 1)                                                         # true reference frame for each query frame
qry = np.stack([np.interp(true_i, np.arange(N), ref[:, k]) for k in range(2)], axis=1) + rng.normal(0, 0.08, (M, 2))
euclid = lambda a, b: np.sqrt(((a - b) ** 2).sum())

def match_error(path):
    matched = np.array([np.mean([i for i, j in path if j == jj]) for jj in range(M)])
    return np.abs(matched - true_i).mean(), np.abs(matched - true_i).max()

results = {}
for name, band in [("full table", None), ("band of 20 frames", 20), ("band of 5 frames", 5)]:
    c, p, cells = dtw(ref, qry, euclid, band)
    results[name] = p
    e_mean, e_max = match_error(p)
    print(f"DTW, {name:18s}: cost {c:7.2f}; {cells:6d} of {N * M} cells; frame error mean {e_mean:.2f}, max {e_max:.2f}")
lin = np.arange(M) * (N - 1) / (M - 1)
print(f"linear stretch (no DTW)    : frame error mean {np.abs(lin - true_i).mean():.2f}, max {np.abs(lin - true_i).max():.2f}")

3. Forced alignment: Viterbi over a CTC trellis¶

To caption a lecture we already have the transcript (or a corrected recognizer output) and need each word's time. A CTC model (session 2) gives, at every frame, probabilities for the blank and every word. Forced alignment finds the single most likely frame path that spells exactly the known transcript: the Viterbi algorithm, the same dynamic program as the CTC forward pass with a maximum in place of the sum. The states are the transcript with blanks around every word, - w1 - w2 - ... - wL -, and at each frame the path may stay, move one state, or skip a blank between two different words.

The posteriors here are synthetic: a short lecture transcript, written for this notebook, with known word start times, 20 ms frames (the frame rate of wav2vec 2.0 models), a short burst for each word near its start, random false bursts, and some weak true bursts. We compare greedy recognition, forced alignment, and a naive alignment that spreads the words over the speech in proportion to their length.

LECTURE = (
    "[music] Welcome back, everyone. Today we are going to talk about captions, and why they matter for deaf and hard of "
    "hearing viewers. Um, a caption is not just the words. It also tells you who is speaking, and it describes sounds "
    "that carry meaning, like music, laughter or a door closing. So when you make captions, you need three things. "
    "First, the words must be right. Second, they must appear at the right time, not two seconds late. And third, they "
    "must be easy to read. That last one is harder than it sounds. If people speak fast, uh, really fast, like I am "
    "doing right now to show you the problem, then the captions change so quickly that you know, nobody can read them "
    "before they disappear from the screen and the next ones arrive. So today we will build a tool that splits a "
    "transcript into short blocks. Each block has at most two lines. Each line has at most forty two characters. And "
    "each block stays on the screen long enough to be read. We will also try not to break a line in the middle of a "
    "phrase, because a break after a word like the or of makes the text harder to follow. "
    "STUDENT: Can we use the same rules for Bangla captions? "

is_cue = [t.startswith("[") for t in tokens]
is_label = [t.endswith(":") and t.isupper() for t in tokens]
fast = np.zeros(len(tokens), bool)

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
start, end, tnow = np.zeros(len(tokens)), np.zeros(len(tokens)), 0.5
for k, tok in enumerate(tokens):
    if is_cue[k]:
        start[k], end[k] = tnow, tnow + 3.0; tnow += 3.3; continue
    if is_label[k]:
        start[k] = end[k] = tnow; continue                                        # labels are not spoken
    rate = 4.2 if fast[k] else 2.6                                                # words per second
    dur = (len(tok) + 2) / 7.0 / rate * rng.uniform(0.85, 1.15)
    start[k], end[k] = tnow, tnow + dur
    tnow += dur + (rng.uniform(0.35, 0.6) if tok[-1] in ".?!" else rng.uniform(0.1, 0.25) if tok[-1] in ",:" else 0.02)
spoken = [k for k in range(len(tokens)) if not (is_cue[k] or is_label[k])]

# synthetic CTC log-probabilities over the blank (column 0) and every word type, at 20 ms frames
FR = 0.02
types = sorted(set(norm(tokens[k]) for k in spoken)); tid = {w: i + 1 for i, w in enumerate(types)}
T = int(np.ceil(tnow / FR)) + 10
logits = rng.normal(0, 1, (T, len(types) + 1)) - 2.0
logits[:, 0] += 8.0
weak = 0
for k in spoken:
    f0 = int(start[k] / FR) + rng.integers(0, 3)                                  # burst 0-40 ms after the word starts
    boost = 9.0 if rng.random() > 0.1 else 5.0                                    # 10% of bursts are weak
    weak += boost < 9
    logits[f0:f0 + rng.integers(1, 3), tid[norm(tokens[k])]] += boost
for f in rng.choice(T, size=int(0.4 * tnow), replace=False):                      # false bursts, 0.4 per second
    logits[f, rng.integers(1, len(types) + 1)] += 9.0
logp = logits - np.log(np.exp(logits).sum(1, keepdims=True))

best = logp.argmax(1)
greedy = [types[b - 1] for i, b in enumerate(best) if b != 0 and (i == 0 or b != best[i - 1])]
ref_words = [norm(tokens[k]) for k in spoken]

def edit_distance(a, b):
    d = list(range(len(b) + 1))
    for i in range(1, len(a) + 1):
        prev, d[0] = d[0], i
        for j in range(1, len(b) + 1):
            cur = min(d[j] + 1, d[j - 1] + 1, prev + (a[i - 1] != b[j - 1]))
            prev, d[j] = d[j], cur
    return d[len(b)]

print(f"greedy recognition from these posteriors: {len(greedy)} words for {len(ref_words)} spoken; "
      f"WER {edit_distance(ref_words, greedy) / len(ref_words):.3f} ({weak} weak bursts)")

def force_align(logp, label_ids, blank=0):
    # Viterbi over the CTC trellis; returns the first frame of every label
    z = [blank]
    for l in label_ids:
        z += [l, blank]
    z = np.array(z); S, T = len(z), len(logp)
    emit = logp[:, z]                                                            # (T, S)
    can_skip = np.zeros(S, bool); can_skip[2:] = (z[2:] != blank) & (z[2:] != z[:-2])
    dp = np.full(S, -np.inf); dp[0], dp[1] = emit[0, 0], emit[0, 1]
    back = np.zeros((T, S), np.int8)
    for t in range(1, T):
        stay, move = dp, np.concatenate([[-np.inf], dp[:-1]])
        skip = np.where(can_skip, np.concatenate([[-np.inf, -np.inf], dp[:-2]]), -np.inf)
        choice = np.argmax(np.stack([stay, move, skip]), axis=0)
        dp = np.stack([stay, move, skip])[choice, np.arange(S)] + emit[t]
        back[t] = choice
    s = S - 1 if dp[S - 1] >= dp[S - 2] else S - 2
    states = np.zeros(T, int)
    for t in range(T - 1, -1, -1):
        states[t] = s
        s -= int(back[t, s])
    return np.array([np.argmax(states == 2 * k + 1) for k in range(len(label_ids))])

4. Splitting a timed transcript into caption blocks¶

Now the words have times. A caption file is a list of blocks: one or two lines of text with a start and an end time. We use example limits: at most 42 characters per line, 2 lines per block, 1 to 7 seconds on screen, and a target reading speed of at most 17 cps. These are illustrative; broadcasters and streaming services publish their own limits, so check the guideline you must follow. A block's end is stretched into the following pause by up to 0.5 s, which lowers its reading speed. Sound cues such as [music] get their own block, and a change of speaker starts a new block.

  • The greedy segmenter fills each line until the next word does not fit, then starts a new line or block.
  • The dynamic-programming (DP) segmenter considers every way to cut the words into blocks and picks the cheapest. A cut after a sentence end costs 0, after a comma 1, after a word that binds to the next one ("the", "of", "to", "and", ...) 10, elsewhere 4; each block costs 2 more, and every cps over 17 adds 3. Inside a block it chooses the cheapest line break.
MAX_CHARS, MAX_LINES, MIN_DUR, MAX_DUR, MAX_CPS = 42, 2, 1.0, 7.0, 17.0
BINDING = set("the a an of to in on for and or but with at by from is are be not we you i it this that my your our "
              "their like can will must has have so if when because than as just into about very really".split())
hard = np.zeros(len(tokens) + 1, bool)                                           # a block must end before these tokens
for k in range(len(tokens)):
    if is_cue[k] or is_label[k]:
        hard[k] = True
    if is_cue[k]:
        hard[k + 1] = True

def cut_cost(k):
    # cost of a break after token k
    w = tokens[k]
    if w[-1] in ".?!" or is_cue[k]:
        return 0.0
    if w[-1] in ",;:":
        return 1.0
    return 10.0 if norm(w) in BINDING else 4.0

def block_timing(i, j):
    t0 = start[i]
    nxt = start[j] - 0.05 if j < len(tokens) else end[j - 1] + 0.5
    return t0, max(end[j - 1], min(nxt, end[j - 1] + 0.5))

def best_lines(i, j):
    # cheapest way to put tokens i..j-1 on at most two lines; returns (cost, lines) or None if impossible
    words = tokens[i:j]
    one = " ".join(words)
    options = [(0.0, [one])] if len(one) <= MAX_CHARS else []
    for k in range(1, len(words)):
        a, b = " ".join(words[:k]), " ".join(words[k:])
        if len(a) <= MAX_CHARS and len(b) <= MAX_CHARS:
            options.append((cut_cost(i + k - 1) * 0.5 + 0.02 * abs(len(a) - len(b)), [a, b]))
    return min(options, key=lambda o: o[0]) if options else None

def block_cost(i, j):
    if hard[i + 1:j].any():
        return None
    lines = best_lines(i, j)
    t0, t1 = block_timing(i, j)
    if lines is None or end[j - 1] - t0 > MAX_DUR or t1 - t0 <= 0:
        return None
    cps = len(" ".join(lines[1])) / (t1 - t0)
    return cut_cost(j - 1) + lines[0] + 2.0 + 3.0 * max(0.0, cps - MAX_CPS) + (5.0 if t1 - t0 < MIN_DUR else 0.0), lines[1]

def segment_dp():
    n = len(tokens)
    best, arg = np.full(n + 1, np.inf), np.zeros(n + 1, int); best[0] = 0.0
    for j in range(1, n + 1):
        for i in range(max(0, j - 20), j):
            if best[i] < np.inf:
                bc = block_cost(i, j)
                if bc is not None and best[i] + bc[0] < best[j]:
                    best[j], arg[j] = best[i] + bc[0], i
    cuts, j = [], n
    while j > 0:
        cuts.append((arg[j], j)); j = arg[j]
    return [(i, j, block_cost(i, j)[1]) for i, j in cuts[::-1]]

def segment_greedy():
    blocks, i, lines, cur = [], 0, [], []
    for k, w in enumerate(tokens):
        if cur and (hard[k] or end[k] - start[i] > MAX_DUR):             # a cue, a new speaker, or a block that is too long
            blocks.append((i, k, lines + [" ".join(cur)])); i, lines, cur = k, [], []
        elif cur and len(" ".join(cur + [w])) > MAX_CHARS:               # the line is full
            lines.append(" ".join(cur)); cur = []
            if len(lines) == MAX_LINES:
                blocks.append((i, k, lines)); i, lines = k, []
        cur.append(w)
    blocks.append((i, len(tokens), lines + [" ".join(cur)]))
    return blocks

def report(name, blocks):
    cps = np.array([len(" ".join(L)) / (block_timing(i, j)[1] - block_timing(i, j)[0]) for i, j, L in blocks])
    cuts = [i + len(L[0].split()) - 1 for i, j, L in blocks if len(L) == 2] + [j - 1 for i, j, L in blocks[:-1]]
    bad = sum(norm(tokens[k]) in BINDING and tokens[k][-1] not in ".,?!:;" for k in cuts)
    print(f"{name:20s}: {len(blocks):3d} blocks; mean {cps.mean():.1f} cps; {bad:2d} line or block breaks right after a binding word; "
          f"{np.sum(cps > MAX_CPS)} blocks over {MAX_CPS:.0f} cps: {', '.join(f'{c:.1f}' for c in cps[cps > MAX_CPS])}")
    return cps

B_greedy, B_dp = segment_greedy(), segment_dp()
cps_g, cps_d = report("greedy", B_greedy), report("dynamic programming", B_dp)

5. Writing SubRip (.srt) captions¶

SubRip is the simplest caption format: a block number, a time line start --> end with milliseconds after a comma, one or two text lines, and a blank line. Most video players and YouTube accept it.

def srt_time(t):
    h, rem = divmod(int(round(t * 1000)), 3600000)
    m, rem = divmod(rem, 60000)
    s, ms = divmod(rem, 1000)
    return f"{h:02d}:{m:02d}:{s:02d},{ms:03d}"

def to_srt(blocks):
    out = []
    for n, (i, j, lines) in enumerate(blocks, 1):
        t0, t1 = block_timing(i, j)
        out.append(f"{n}\n{srt_time(t0)} --> {srt_time(t1)}\n" + "\n".join(lines) + "\n")
    return "\n".join(out)

# to save it: open("lecture.srt", "w", encoding="utf-8").write(srt)

6. Word timestamps from real audio (NOT RUN here)¶

WhisperX (Bain et al. 2023) runs Whisper on voice-activity segments and then force-aligns the words with a wav2vec 2.0 CTC model, as in section 3. Its alignment models are language-specific. In version 3.8.6 (checked in November 2026) its list of default alignment models has no Bangla entry, so for Bangla you must pass model_name= with a Bangla wav2vec 2.0 CTC model you have tested. Plain Whisper also gives rough word times with word_timestamps=True.

# NOT RUN: needs whisperx (pip install whisperx), ffmpeg, a GPU for reasonable speed, model downloads and a video file
import whisperx
device, audio_file = "cuda", "lecture.mp4"
model = whisperx.load_model("large-v2", device, compute_type="float16")
audio = whisperx.load_audio(audio_file)
result = model.transcribe(audio, batch_size=16)
model_a, metadata = whisperx.load_align_model(language_code=result["language"], device=device)   # for Bangla add model_name=...
result = whisperx.align(result["segments"], model_a, metadata, audio, device, return_char_alignments=False)
words = [(w["word"], w["start"], w["end"]) for seg in result["segments"] for w in seg["words"] if "start" in w]
print(words[:10])     # feed these into the segmenter of section 4 in place of the synthetic times

What to notice¶

(Written from the run above; your numbers will match if you keep the seeds.)

  1. Reading speed adds up quickly. A 43-character block shown for 2.0 seconds asks for 21.5 cps; at 17 cps it needs 2.53 s. In the toy DTW example the cost was 0: frame 0 of x matched frames 0 and 1 of y, and frame 2 matched frames 3 and 4.
  2. DTW recovers a non-linear warp, and a band saves work. The full table and a 20-frame Sakoe–Chiba band found the same path (cost 15.18, mean error 0.53 frames, worst 2.27), but the band filled only 4,500 of 19,200 cells. A 5-frame band was too narrow, because the true warp leaves it: the cost rose to 75.99 and the mean error to 3.70 frames (worst 9.66). Stretching linearly, with no DTW, gave a mean error of 6.02 frames.
  3. Knowing the transcript makes alignment easy. Greedy recognition from the same posteriors was poor (224 words for 253 spoken, WER 0.300), yet forced alignment placed the word starts with a median error of 15 ms (95th percentile 44 ms, worst 153 ms) in a fraction of a second. Spreading the words in proportion to their length gave a median error of 815 ms and a worst error of 3.7 s. The 15 ms median partly reflects how we built the posteriors: each burst starts 0 to 40 ms after its word.
  4. Dynamic programming kept phrases together, but did not win on reading speed. Both segmenters averaged 13.3 cps. The greedy segmenter made 19 blocks with 19 breaks right after words like "the", "of" or "to"; the DP segmenter made 21 blocks with 1 such break. Against our expectation, the DP produced more blocks over 17 cps (3 blocks: 17.3, 20.1 and 20.2 cps) than greedy (2 blocks: 20.8 and 21.9 cps). All of them fall in the fast passage (about 4 words per second), which no segmentation can show word for word at 17 cps; the DP cut it into three blocks at phrase boundaries, which lowered the worst block from 21.9 to 20.2 cps. Different cost weights give a different trade-off, and the weights should come from testing with viewers.
  5. The output is an ordinary .srt file. The first block is the sound cue [music] from 00:00:00,500 to 00:00:03,750, and the DP's blocks end at sentence ends or commas.
