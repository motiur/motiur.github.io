Ten Years of Deep Learning Ideas

The big deep learning ideas of 2016–2026 that the other courses don't teach: residuals, normalization and µP, modern optimizers, the generalization puzzles, vision transformers, self-supervised and in-context learning, VAEs to GANs, diffusion and flow matching, deep RL, neural fields, and robustness and calibration.

  • Optimizers
  • Self-supervised learning
  • Diffusion & flow matching
  • Deep RL
  • Neural fields

Sessions

  1. 01

    Making depth work: residuals, normalization and µP

    Measure how signals vanish or explode in deep networks, fix them with residual connections and normalization, and run a µP coordinate check and a learning-rate sweep across widths.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  2. 02

    Optimizers, schedules and training at scale

    Compare Adam with L2 regularization against AdamW, test warmup and three learning-rate schedules, write Lion and Muon from scratch, and estimate the memory needed to train a large model.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  3. 03

    The generalization puzzles

    Reproduce memorization of random labels, double descent, the edge of stability, sharpness-aware minimization and lottery tickets at toy scale, and say what each result does and does not show.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb Runs fully on CPU
  4. 04

    Vision transformers and the modern ConvNet

    Turn images into patch tokens, train a tiny vision transformer and a CNN with the same parameter budget at two training-set sizes, and measure what mixup and label smoothing change.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  5. 05

    Self-supervised learning

    Train SimCLR, SimSiam and a tiny masked autoencoder without labels, detect collapse, and compare their features with a supervised model using a linear probe on 10% of the labels.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  6. 06

    In-context learning and learning to learn

    Train prototypical networks and first-order MAML on few-shot tasks, then train a tiny transformer to do linear regression in context and compare it with least squares and one step of gradient descent.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  7. 07

    Generative models before diffusion: VAEs, VQ-VAE, flows and GANs

    Train a VAE, a VQ-VAE, a coupling flow and two GANs at toy scale, and measure their samples with likelihoods, mode counts, Fréchet distance, precision and recall.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  8. 08

    Diffusion models

    Train a denoising diffusion model on 2-D data, sample it with DDPM and DDIM at several step counts, and measure the fidelity-diversity trade-off of classifier-free guidance.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  9. 09

    Flow matching, rectified flow and fast generation

    Train a flow-matching model on the same 2-D data as session 8, sample it with 1 to 32 Euler steps, and measure how reflow and minibatch optimal-transport pairing straighten its paths.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  10. 10

    Deep reinforcement learning, from AlphaGo to world models

    Write CartPole from its equations, train an agent on it with PPO and GAE, test what PPO's clipping does, and play tic-tac-toe with Monte Carlo tree search.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  11. 11

    Neural fields and 3D, from Fourier features to Gaussian splatting

    Fit an image with ReLU, Fourier-feature and SIREN coordinate networks, then fit a toy 2-D radiance field by differentiable volume rendering and render a view it never saw.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  12. 12

    Robustness, uncertainty and calibration

    Attack a small digit classifier with FGSM and PGD, train it adversarially, then measure its calibration, a deep ensemble, conformal prediction sets and what happens to all of them under a distribution shift.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell

Before you start

One notebook per session. Read them with their saved outputs on the course website or on GitHub, or open them in Google Colab or Kaggle Notebooks and run them from the top.

  • Every cell without a NOT RUN label was run once, top to bottom, on a CPU in November 2026 (Python 3.13, PyTorch 2.15, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn 1.9, matplotlib 3.11), and the outputs are saved in the notebooks. A second, independent run reproduced every printed number; only timings differ.
  • The runnable parts use small built-in or synthetic data (scikit-learn's digits, two moons, swiss roll and sample image, or data generated in the notebook), so each notebook finishes in about two minutes or less on a CPU. The numbers show how a method behaves; they are not benchmark results.
  • Several notebooks call torch.set_num_threads(1). For these small models it is as fast as more threads and keeps results identical between runs; remove it if you move a lab to a GPU.
  • Cells marked TODO are exercises for students. Cells marked NOT RUN need a GPU, a download, an account or a library that is not installed here. They were written from the libraries' documented interfaces and have not been executed, so run each one before recording (the table below says what each needs).
  • Nothing extra needs installing for the runnable cells on Google Colab or Kaggle.
  • Results that went against the papers are stated in each notebook's "What to notice" cell and in the course document. Examples: a vanilla GAN covered 4 of 8 modes while WGAN-GP covered 7–8 (session 7); DDIM was not clearly better than DDPM at few steps on 2-D data (session 8); PPO without clipping crashed only at a deliberately large learning rate (session 10); temperature scaling barely changed the calibration error, and the ensemble's calibration error was worse than a single model's (session 12).
