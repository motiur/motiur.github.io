Deep Learning on Curved Spaces

Riemannian geometry for machine learning, built from the sphere upward and checked in code: manifolds, geodesics, curvature and Riemannian optimization, then hyperbolic embeddings and networks, SPD matrices for EEG, rotations and Lie groups, generative models on manifolds and mixed-curvature spaces.

  • Riemannian geometry
  • Hyperbolic networks
  • SPD matrices
  • Rotations
  • Flow matching on manifolds

Sessions

  1. 01

    Why curved spaces?

    Compute circular means, great-circle distances, log-Euclidean means of SPD matrices and slerp of rotations, and measure how the Euclidean versions go wrong.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  2. 02

    Manifolds, tangent spaces and metrics

    Describe the sphere by a constraint and by charts, compute tangent spaces, projections and metric tensors, and measure curve lengths, latitude circles and Mercator's distortion with them.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  3. 03

    Geodesics, exponential maps and the Fréchet mean

    Implement the sphere's exponential and logarithm maps and parallel transport, find Fréchet means by Riemannian gradient descent, and measure holonomy around geodesic triangles.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  4. 04

    Curvature and hyperbolic space

    Measure curvature with circles and triangles, compute distances in the Poincaré disk and the upper half-plane, embed a tree in the hyperbolic plane by Sarkar's construction, and measure its distortion and its precision cost.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb Runs fully on CPU
  5. 05

    Riemannian optimization

    Compute Riemannian gradients and retractions on the sphere, the Stiefel manifold and the orthogonal group, and use them for eigenvectors, principal subspaces and Procrustes alignment, and to test optimizers with momentum and Adam.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  6. 06

    Computing in hyperbolic space: the Poincaré and Lorentz models

    Implement Möbius addition, distances, exponential and logarithm maps in the Poincaré ball with curvature −c and in the Lorentz model, convert between them, and measure where float32 and float64 break.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  7. 07

    Hyperbolic embeddings of hierarchies

    Train Poincaré embeddings of a hierarchy with Riemannian SGD, and compare them with Euclidean embeddings trained with the same loss and with session 4's constructive embedding, using mean average precision and distortion.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  8. 08

    Hyperbolic neural networks

    Build hyperbolic layers from exp/log maps and Möbius operations and check them numerically, test graphs for tree-likeness with Gromov's δ, and compare a hyperbolic MLP with a Euclidean MLP of the same size on data whose labels follow a tree.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  9. 09

    SPD matrices: the geometry of covariance

    Compute Euclidean, log-Euclidean, affine-invariant and Bures–Wasserstein distances and means of SPD matrices, check their invariances, and use them to classify synthetic EEG-like covariance matrices within and across subjects.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  10. 10

    Rotations and poses: the Lie groups SO(3) and SE(3)

    Compute exp and log maps of SO(3) and SE(3) and check them against SciPy, measure rotation errors by geodesic distance, compare Euler angles, quaternions and the 6-D representation for regressing rotations with a neural network, and average rotations correctly.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb Runs fully on CPU
  11. 11

    Generative models on manifolds

    Sample from von Mises–Fisher and wrapped distributions on the sphere with the right volume measure, train a Riemannian flow matching model whose samples stay on the sphere, and compare it with Euclidean flow matching followed by normalization.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb Runs fully on CPU
  12. 12

    Mixed curvature, choosing a geometry, and the capstone

    Diagnose the geometry of a graph with δ-hyperbolicity and a triangle-based curvature estimate, embed it in Euclidean, hyperbolic, spherical and product spaces with Riemannian Adam, and compare the geometries fairly across seeds.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell

Before you start

One notebook per session. Read them with their saved outputs on the course website or on GitHub, or open them in Google Colab or Kaggle Notebooks and run them from the top.

  • Every cell without a NOT RUN label was run once, top to bottom, on a CPU in November 2026 (Python 3.13, NumPy 2.5, SciPy 1.18, scikit-learn 1.9, networkx 3.7 and matplotlib 3.11, plus PyTorch 2.15 in sessions 5–8 and 10–12), and the outputs are saved in the notebooks. A second, independent run reproduced every printed number; only timings differ.
  • The geometry is implemented by hand in NumPy and PyTorch (exponential and logarithm maps, Möbius operations, Riemannian gradients, SPD means, rotation maps), and every formula is checked numerically in the notebook: round trips, identities, and comparisons with SciPy. The data are synthetic (trees, directions on the sphere, EEG-like covariance matrices, rotated point clouds) or generated by networkx, so each notebook finishes in under a minute on a CPU.
  • Cells marked TODO are exercises for students. Cells marked NOT RUN show the same steps with libraries that are not installed here (geoopt, geomstats, pyRiemann, nltk's WordNet, MOABB); they were written from the libraries' documented interfaces and have not been executed, so run each one before recording (the table below says what each needs).
  • Nothing extra needs installing for the runnable cells on Google Colab or Kaggle.
  • Results that went against expectation are stated in each notebook's "What to notice" cell and in the course document. Examples: Sarkar's tree embedding with short edges did worse than a flat layout, because branches overlapped (session 4); in 10 dimensions a Euclidean embedding of the tree matched or beat the hyperbolic one, while in 2 dimensions the hyperbolic one always won (session 7); a Euclidean MLP beat the hyperbolic MLP at every size we tried (session 8); after recentring each subject's data, all four SPD classifiers, Euclidean included, reached about the same accuracy (session 9); Riemannian flow matching matched the data much better than Euclidean flow matching but leaked more mass between clusters (session 11); in the mixed-curvature embeddings the sphere factor became almost flat and the circle factor collapsed in two of three runs (session 12).
