AI Research in 2026

What AI research found from January to November 2026: agents' time horizons, RL for reasoning, multi-turn reliability, diffusion language models, transformer expressivity, Polar Express and Muon, provable grokking, high-accuracy sampling, deception probes, AI on Erdős problems and 4D reconstruction, each with a toy lab.

Sessions

  1. 01

    The year in AI research, and how to read it

    Compute confidence intervals for benchmark scores, compare two models on the same questions with a paired bootstrap, measure how much "try 20 prompts and report the best" inflates a result, and build a claims ledger.

  2. 02

    Agents and long tasks: measuring time horizons

    Fit an agent's 50% and 80% time horizons from simulated task results, put bootstrap intervals on them, estimate a doubling time, and see how a capped task suite and scoring rules bias the estimates.

  3. 03

    Reasoning with reinforcement learning: exploration and entropy

    Train a toy policy with GRPO-style updates and PPO clipping, track entropy, pass@1 and pass@k, and compare spurious rewards and exploration-preserving variants.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  4. 04

    Lost in conversation: multi-turn reliability

    Run the sharded-conversation evaluation protocol on simulated assistants and measure aptitude, unreliability and the gap between single-turn and multi-turn performance.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  5. 05

    Diffusion language models and the flexibility trap

    Train a tiny masked diffusion model on a task with several valid solutions and compare left-to-right, confidence-first and random decoding orders on accuracy, diversity and pass@k.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb 1 NOT RUN cell
  6. 06

    What transformers can express

    Compute minimal automaton sizes by brute force, build an attention-based recognizer by hand, compare local and global attention on two small languages, and test whether a trained transformer finds a solution that generalizes.

    Read notebook Colab Kaggle .ipynb Runs fully on CPU
  7. 07

    Optimizers in 2026: Polar Express and Muon

  8. 08

    Grokking, proved

    Reproduce grokking in over-parameterized ridge regression, predict the grokking time with one line of algebra, and show how weight decay, learning rate and initialization stretch or remove it.

  9. 09

    Sampling with high accuracy

  10. 10

    Probes, obfuscation and honesty

Before you start

One notebook per session. Read them with their saved outputs on the course website or on GitHub, or open them in Google Colab or Kaggle Notebooks and run them from the top.

  • Each notebook is a toy illustration of one 2026 paper's or report's central idea, named in its first section. It is not a reproduction, and its numbers say nothing about the paper's benchmarks.
  • Every cell without a NOT RUN label was run once, top to bottom, on a CPU in November 2026 (Python 3.13, PyTorch 2.15, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn 1.9, matplotlib 3.11), and the outputs are saved in the notebooks. A second, independent run reproduced every printed number; only timings differ.
  • The runnable parts use synthetic data generated in the notebook, so each notebook finishes in under two minutes on a CPU.
  • Cells marked TODO are exercises for students. Cells marked NOT RUN need a GPU, a download, a model or internet access (for example to fetch a paper's released code or data). They were written from documented interfaces and have not been executed; check that the links still work and run each one before recording.
  • Nothing extra needs installing for the runnable cells on Google Colab or Kaggle.
  • Results that went against the papers are stated in each notebook's "What to notice" cell and in the course document. Examples: a higher upper clipping bound made no measurable difference to entropy in the RLVR toy (session 3); the "flexibility trap" toy showed the drop with small transformers but the opposite with an MLP denoiser, so it does not isolate the paper's mechanism (session 5); training against a probe with REINFORCE gave no obfuscated-activation runs, unlike the paper (session 10); per-scene fitting was more accurate than the feed-forward model, partly because it was given the true form of the world (session 12).
  • Facts in the notebooks were checked in November 2026. Re-check them after NeurIPS 2026, as the course document's update checklist describes.
