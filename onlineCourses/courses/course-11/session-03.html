1. By hand first: eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a 2 × 2 matrix¶

An eigenvector of a square matrix $A$ is a nonzero direction that $A$ only stretches: $Av = \lambda v$, where the number $\lambda$ is its eigenvalue. The eigenvalues solve $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$. For a symmetric matrix ($A = A^\top$) the eigenvalues are real and the eigenvectors can be chosen orthonormal (the spectral theorem), so $A = V \Lambda V^\top$.

By hand first: for $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ (the matrix $M$ of session 1), solve $(2 - \lambda)^2 - 1 = 0$, then find a unit eigenvector for each eigenvalue and check that the two are orthogonal.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from sklearn.datasets import load_digits, load_sample_image
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA

np.random.seed(0)
np.set_printoptions(precision=4, suppress=True)

A = np.array([[2.0, 1.0], [1.0, 2.0]])
lam, V = np.linalg.eigh(A)                    # eigh is for symmetric matrices; it returns eigenvalues in increasing order
print("eigenvalues:", lam)
print("eigenvectors (columns):\n", V)
for i in range(2):
    print(f"A v{i + 1} = {A @ V[:, i]},  lambda{i + 1} v{i + 1} = {lam[i] * V[:, i]}")
print("V^T V =\n", V.T @ V, "\nV diag(lambda) V^T rebuilds A:", np.allclose(V @ np.diag(lam) @ V.T, A))

2. Power iteration¶

Multiply any starting vector by $A$ again and again, normalizing each time. Write the start as a mix of eigenvectors: each multiplication scales the part along $v_i$ by $\lambda_i$, so the part with the largest $|\lambda|$ takes over. The direction error shrinks like $|\lambda_2 / \lambda_1|^k$ after $k$ steps. The Rayleigh quotient $x^\top A x / x^\top x$ estimates $\lambda_1$; for a symmetric matrix its error shrinks like $|\lambda_2/\lambda_1|^{2k}$.

By hand first: start from $x_0 = (1, 0)$ with the matrix above. Compute $x_1 = Ax_0$, $x_2$ and $x_3$ (no normalizing needed for three steps), and the Rayleigh quotient of $x_2 = (5, 4)$.

x = np.array([1.0, 0.0])
for k in range(1, 5):
    x = A @ x
    print(f"x{k} = {x},  Rayleigh quotient {x @ A @ x / (x @ x):.6f}")

x1 = [2. 1.],  Rayleigh quotient 2.800000
x2 = [5. 4.],  Rayleigh quotient 2.975610
x3 = [14. 13.],  Rayleigh quotient 2.997260
x4 = [41. 40.],  Rayleigh quotient 2.999695

3. By hand first: the singular value decomposition¶

Every matrix, square or not, has a singular value decomposition (SVD) $B = U \Sigma V^\top$: $U$ and $V$ have orthonormal columns and $\Sigma$ is diagonal with singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge 0$. The squares $\sigma_i^2$ are the eigenvalues of $B^\top B$, and the columns of $V$ are its eigenvectors. Writing $B = \sum_i \sigma_i u_i v_i^\top$, the truncated SVD $B_k$ keeps the first $k$ terms. Eckart–Young: no matrix of rank $k$ is closer to $B$ in the Frobenius norm, and the error is $\|B - B_k\|_F = \sqrt{\sigma_{k+1}^2 + \sigma_{k+2}^2 + \dots}$.

By hand first: for $B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ compute $B^\top B$, its eigenvalues, and $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_2$. What is the error of the best rank-1 approximation, and what fraction of $\|B\|_F^2 = 9 + 16 + 25$ does it keep?

B = np.array([[3.0, 0.0], [4.0, 5.0]])
print("B^T B =\n", B.T @ B, "\neigenvalues of B^T B:", np.linalg.eigvalsh(B.T @ B))
U, s, Vt = np.linalg.svd(B)
print("singular values:", s, " squares:", s ** 2, f" (sqrt 45 = {np.sqrt(45):.4f}, sqrt 5 = {np.sqrt(5):.4f})")
print("U Sigma V^T rebuilds B:", np.allclose(U @ np.diag(s) @ Vt, B))
B1 = s[0] * np.outer(U[:, 0], Vt[0])
print("best rank-1 approximation:\n", B1, f"\nerror ||B - B1||_F = {np.linalg.norm(B - B1):.4f}; kept {s[0] ** 2 / (s ** 2).sum():.0%} of ||B||_F^2")

4. Image compression with the SVD¶

A grey image is a matrix of brightness values. Storing its rank-$k$ SVD needs $k(m + n + 1)$ numbers instead of $mn$. We compress china.jpg from scikit-learn at several ranks, check the error against Eckart–Young's formula, and compare with another reasonable rank-$k$ method: keep $k$ evenly spaced columns of the image and write every other column as their best least-squares combination (session 2).

img = load_sample_image("china.jpg").astype(float) / 255
gray = img @ np.array([0.299, 0.587, 0.114])          # standard luminance weights for red, green, blue
m, n_cols = gray.shape
U, s, Vt = np.linalg.svd(gray, full_matrices=False)
print(f"image {m} x {n_cols}; largest singular values {np.round(s[:4], 1)}; the first term alone holds {s[0] ** 2 / (s ** 2).sum():.1%} of the squared Frobenius norm")
approx = {}
print("rank   storage   SVD error   Eckart-Young formula   k columns + least squares")
for k in [5, 20, 50, 100]:
    Ak = (U[:, :k] * s[:k]) @ Vt[:k]
    approx[k] = Ak
    err = np.linalg.norm(gray - Ak) / np.linalg.norm(gray)
    formula = np.sqrt((s[k:] ** 2).sum()) / np.linalg.norm(gray)
    cols = np.linspace(0, n_cols - 1, k).astype(int)
    Ck = gray[:, cols]
    err_cols = np.linalg.norm(gray - Ck @ np.linalg.lstsq(Ck, gray, rcond=None)[0]) / np.linalg.norm(gray)
    print(f"{k:4d}   {k * (m + n_cols + 1) / (m * n_cols):6.1%}   {err:9.4f}   {formula:20.4f}   {err_cols:25.4f}")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 4, figsize=(9, 2.2))
for a_, k in zip(ax, [5, 20, 50]):
    a_.imshow(np.clip(approx[k], 0, 1), cmap="gray"); a_.set_title(f"rank {k}", fontsize=9)
ax[3].imshow(gray, cmap="gray"); ax[3].set_title(f"original (rank {min(m, n_cols)})", fontsize=9)
for a_ in ax: a_.axis("off")
plt.suptitle("Truncated SVD of a 427 x 640 grey image (relative errors printed above)", fontsize=9)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

5. PCA is the SVD of centred data¶

Principal component analysis (PCA) looks for the directions in which the data vary most. Centre the data matrix ($X_c = X - \bar x$), take its SVD $X_c = U\Sigma V^\top$:

  • the principal directions are the columns of $V$, the eigenvectors of the covariance $C = X_c^\top X_c / (n - 1)$;
  • the variance along direction $i$ is $\sigma_i^2/(n - 1)$, and the explained-variance ratio is $\sigma_i^2 / \sum_j \sigma_j^2$;
  • the scores (coordinates in the new directions) are $X_c V = U\Sigma$.

We run PCA on the digits with NumPy, compare with scikit-learn, and choose $k$ from the cumulative explained variance.

U_d, s_d, Vt_d = np.linalg.svd(Xc, full_matrices=False)
evr = s_d ** 2 / (s_d ** 2).sum()
print("explained-variance ratio of the first five components:", evr[:5], f"; first two together {evr[:2].sum():.3f}")
print("largest eigenvalue of C from the SVD:", round(s_d[0] ** 2 / (n - 1), 4), " from eigh:", round(lam_all[0], 4))
pca = PCA().fit(X)                                       # scikit-learn centres the data itself
print("scikit-learn agrees:", np.allclose(pca.explained_variance_ratio_, evr),
      "; first 20 components equal up to sign:", np.allclose(np.abs(pca.components_[:20]), np.abs(Vt_d[:20]), atol=1e-8))
print(f"the last {np.sum(s_d < 1e-10 * s_d[0])} singular values are zero (blank pixels), so those directions are arbitrary")
cum = np.cumsum(evr)
for target in [0.5, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99]:
    print(f"components needed for {target:.0%} of the variance: {np.searchsorted(cum, target) + 1}")

scores = Xc @ Vt_d[:2].T
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.5))
ax[0].plot(np.arange(1, 65), cum, marker=".", ms=3)
ax[0].axhline(0.9, color="gray", ls=":"); ax[0].axvline(np.searchsorted(cum, 0.9) + 1, color="gray", ls=":")
ax[0].set(title="Cumulative explained variance", xlabel="number of components k", ylabel="fraction of variance")

6. Reconstructing digits from $k$ components¶

Projecting onto the first $k$ directions and back gives $\hat x = \bar x + V_k V_k^\top (x - \bar x)$. By Eckart–Young, the total squared error over all images is $\sum_{i > k} \sigma_i^2$: the mean squared error per image is that sum divided by $n$.

recon = {}
for k in [2, 10, 30, 64]:
    Vk = Vt_d[:k].T
    recon[k] = mu + (Xc @ Vk) @ Vk.T
    mse = ((X - recon[k]) ** 2).sum(axis=1).mean()
    print(f"k = {k:2d}: mean squared error per image {mse:9.3f}; sum of dropped sigma_i^2 / n = {(s_d[k:] ** 2).sum() / n:9.3f}")

show = [0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]
fig, ax = plt.subplots(4, 8, figsize=(9, 3.5))
for col, i in enumerate(show):
    for row, (label, img_) in enumerate([("original", X[i]), ("k = 2", recon[2][i]), ("k = 10", recon[10][i]), ("k = 30", recon[30][i])]):
        ax[row, col].imshow(img_.reshape(8, 8), cmap="gray_r", vmin=0, vmax=16)
        ax[row, col].set_xticks([]); ax[row, col].set_yticks([])
        if col == 0:
            ax[row, col].set_ylabel(label, fontsize=8)
plt.suptitle("Digits rebuilt from k principal components (rows: original, k = 2, 10, 30)", fontsize=9)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

7. Two misunderstandings, tested¶

  • "PCA needs no centring." Without centring, the first singular vector mostly points at the mean image, because every digit is a positive image near the mean. It describes where the data sit, not how they vary.
  • "PCA keeps the most important original features." Each component is a weighted mix of pixels. PCA builds new directions; it does not select pixels.
_, s_raw, Vt_raw = np.linalg.svd(X, full_matrices=False)          # SVD of the raw, uncentred data
unit_mean = mu / np.linalg.norm(mu)
print(f"|cos| between the first direction and the mean image: uncentred {abs(Vt_raw[0] @ unit_mean):.5f}, centred {abs(Vt_d[0] @ unit_mean):.4f}")

9. Eigenfaces (NOT RUN here)¶

The same PCA on photos of faces gives "eigenfaces", a classic example of how few directions describe a large family of images.

# NOT RUN: needs a download of the Labeled Faces in the Wild photos through fetch_lfw_people.
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from sklearn.datasets import fetch_lfw_people
faces = fetch_lfw_people(min_faces_per_person=70, resize=0.4)
Xf = faces.data                                   # one row per photo, one column per pixel
h, w = faces.images.shape[1:]
mean_face = Xf.mean(axis=0)
_, s_f, Vt_f = np.linalg.svd(Xf - mean_face, full_matrices=False)
evr_f = s_f ** 2 / (s_f ** 2).sum()
print(f"{Xf.shape[0]} photos of {h} x {w} pixels; components for 90% of the variance: {np.searchsorted(np.cumsum(evr_f), 0.9) + 1}")
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 6, figsize=(9, 2.2))
ax[0].imshow(mean_face.reshape(h, w), cmap="gray"); ax[0].set_title("mean face", fontsize=9)
for i in range(5):
    ax[i + 1].imshow(Vt_f[i].reshape(h, w), cmap="gray"); ax[i + 1].set_title(f"eigenface {i + 1}", fontsize=9)

What to notice¶

  • The hand calculations matched. $A$ has eigenvalues 1 and 3 with orthogonal unit eigenvectors along (1, −1) and (1, 1) (eigh lists the smaller eigenvalue first). Power iteration from (1, 0) gave (2, 1), (5, 4), (14, 13), with Rayleigh quotients 2.800000, 2.975610 and 2.997260 approaching 3. For $B$, the singular values were 6.7082 = √45 and 2.2361 = √5, and the best rank-1 approximation (rows (1.5, 1.5) and (4.5, 4.5)) had error 2.2361 = $\sigma_2$, keeping 90% of $\|B\|_F^2$.
  • Power iteration is slow when $\lambda_2$ is close to $\lambda_1$. On the digits covariance $\lambda_2/\lambda_1 = 0.9146$: after 50 steps the eigenvalue error was 4.7e-04 and the direction error 5.6e-03, and only after about 200 steps were both at rounding level. The direction error followed $(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^k$ and the eigenvalue error fell twice as fast on the log scale, as theory says for symmetric matrices.
  • Eckart–Young held exactly (section 4). The relative errors 0.1867, 0.1386, 0.1041 and 0.0742 at ranks 5, 20, 50 and 100 equalled the formula from the dropped singular values, and keeping $k$ columns with their best least-squares mix was worse at every rank (0.2404, 0.1766, 0.1373, 0.1046). Note that the first singular value alone holds 91.4% of the squared norm (mostly overall brightness), so a relative error of 0.14 still looks visibly blurred at rank 20.
  • PCA from the SVD matched scikit-learn (sections 5–6). The first two components hold 0.285 of the variance, which is why many digits overlap in the 2-D scatter; 21 components hold 90%, 29 hold 95% and 41 hold 99%. The last 3 singular values are zero because 3 pixels are blank in every image. The reconstruction errors (858.945, 314.515 and 49.158 per image for $k$ = 2, 10, 30) equalled the dropped $\sigma_i^2 / n$.
  • The two misunderstandings failed the test (section 7). Without centring, the first direction had $|\cos|$ = 0.99998 with the mean image (0.0064 after centring) and a misleading "explained variance" of 0.696 instead of 0.149. The first principal component gave weight to 61 of the 64 pixels, and 32 pixels had weights above 0.05 in size: it is a mixture, not a selection.
  • Small data on one CPU; for large matrices libraries use randomized or iterative SVD methods, but the mathematics is the same.
